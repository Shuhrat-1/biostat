Randomized block ANOVA (RCBD)

Do the treatments differ when the experiment is divided into blocks — parts of a field, days, batches — and each treatment appears exactly once in every block? ANOVA with blocks removes the differences between blocks from the error, and the comparison of treatments becomes more precise.

Idea

In a heterogeneous field, the yield of a plot depends both on the treatment and on where the plot lies. In an RCBD the plots are grouped into blocks that are alike inside, and in each block every treatment gets one randomly chosen plot (Welham, p. 58). The block effect can then be estimated and removed (Welham, p. 129–132).

The sum of squares splits into three parts: blocks, treatments and residual. Treatments and blocks are orthogonal, so the treatment means do not depend on the block effects (Welham, p. 134–139). The block is a structural factor: its F shows how much the blocks differed, while the question of the experiment is about the treatments.

Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths

  • More precise with heterogeneous units: the differences between blocks leave the error (Welham, p. 57–58, 134–139). Witte shows the same principle for repeated measures: accounting for subjects raised F from 7.36 to 27 on the same numbers (p. 331–332). But those are repeated measures, not blocks, with their own conditions: the order of conditions and carry-over (p. 325), sphericity (p. 336).
  • Treatment means do not depend on the blocks: the design is orthogonal, and the ANOVA table does not depend on the order of the terms (Welham, p. 134–139).
  • Everything for comparisons: means with CIs, SEM, SED, LSD (Welham, p. 140–141).
  • Transforming the response — in the same form (log, √, logit): means and CIs on the original scale, for log — ratios [BioStat].
  • Blocks also work in observational studies — for example, pairs of fields on several farms (Welham, p. 129–132).

Weaknesses

  • A complete design is required: each treatment exactly once in every block. A missing value, a duplicate or an incomplete block — BioStat stops with an error (Welham, p. 58) [BioStat].
  • The block × treatment interaction cannot be separated from the error: if the treatments respond differently to whatever distinguishes the blocks, an RCBD is inappropriate (Welham, p. 131, 139).
  • Several measurements of one plot are not replicates: they are analyzed by ANOVA with subsampling; otherwise the residual is too small and the df are inflated (Welham, p. 141–146).
  • The method's pairwise comparisons have no multiplicity adjustment (LSD) [BioStat].

Welham et al. (2015), p. 57–58, 129–146; Witte & Witte (2017), p. 325, 331–332, 336; [BioStat].

Assumptions

AssumptionWhat it meansHow BioStat checks it
Randomization within blocksIn each block the treatments are assigned to plots at random, independently of the other blocks (Welham, p. 58).Not checked by calculation — it is a property of the design.
Additivity of blocks and treatmentsThe differences between treatments are the same in all blocks (Welham, p. 131).Not checked: with one plot per treatment in a block, the interaction cannot be separated from the residual.
Normality and homogeneity of residualsThe residuals of the model “treatment + block” are normal, and their spread is the same for all treatments.The method draws residual plots (Welham, p. 137). The map checks normality and homogeneity of variances on the residuals of the same model “treatment + block” [BioStat].
No gross recording errorsA single plot does not distort the means and the residual.Plots with |standardized residual| > 2 — a note: check against the original records, do not remove without a found cause (Welham, p. 109).

Welham et al. (2015), p. 58, 109, 131, 137.

How the map chooses this method

Path to the leaf

Analyse a response›Continuous›Differences›Independent units›No blocks or complete blocks›One factor›Three or more groups›ANOVA with blocks (RCBD)

The map leads here with signal B1.2: a column has the role “block”, and each treatment occurs in each block exactly once (Welham, p. 58). If the file has a column that looks like blocks but has no role, the hint B1.8 appears: an analysis without that column is wrong.

Other block structures lead to neighboring leaves: several rows in a block × treatment cell — B1.3, incomplete blocks — B1.4, rows and columns — B1.5, unequal replication or empty cells — B1.7.

In a complete block experiment the assumption checks lead here or to Friedman: SDs grow with the means or residuals are skewed (B4.3, B4.4) — this same ANOVA with the “Transformation” parameter; variances are heterogeneous without a link to the means (B4.5) — a hint on this leaf: there is no Welch analogue with blocks; residuals are non-normal with homogeneous variances, or the response is a score (B4.6, B4.8) — a hint towards the Friedman test. With blocks of another structure these checks stay silent — the B1 signals lead.

What next

A significant F says that the treatments differ; the method itself gives all pairs without an adjustment (LSD). For comparisons with an adjustment — Tukey, or Dunnett against a control — the comparisons of means of the “After ANOVA” step with the same block column [BioStat].
