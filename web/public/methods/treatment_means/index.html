Group means with SED and LSD

What are the treatment means, and how precisely is the difference between any two of them estimated? The method gives the means with SEM and CIs from the common ANOVA variance, the SED and the LSD — the least significant difference.

Idea

After ANOVA the variance within groups is estimated from all the data at once — this is MSWMS_W (in the book s2s^2) on N − t df. It is used for every mean: SEM = √(s²/n), not the SD of its own group. The estimate is more stable, and its df are larger than those of a single group (Welham, p. 84–85).

For a difference of two means the error is SED; it is always larger than SEM. SED multiplied by the t quantile is LSD: a difference larger than LSD is significant at 5 % if it is a single comparison chosen in advance (Welham, p. 85–88). For many pairs in a row, LSD without an adjustment gives too many false findings (Welham, p. 195–199).

Means and their CIs

SEMj=s2/nj=3.91,yˉj⋅±tN−t[0.025]⋅SEMj,t16[0.025]=2.120\mathrm{SEM}_j = \sqrt{s^2/n_j} = 3.91,\qquad \bar y_{j\cdot} \pm t_{N-t}[0.025]\cdot\mathrm{SEM}_j,\qquad t_{16}[0.025] = 2.120

s2=MSWs^2 = MS_W = 76.35 on N − t = 16 df (Welham, p. 84–85).

SED and LSD

SEDjk=s2(1nj+1nk)=5.53,LSD=tN−t[0.025]⋅SED=11.72\mathrm{SED}_{jk} = \sqrt{s^2\left(\frac1{n_j}+\frac1{n_k}\right)} = 5.53,\qquad \mathrm{LSD} = t_{N-t}[0.025]\cdot\mathrm{SED} = 11.72

With equal replication, SED and LSD are the same for all pairs. CI of a difference: (yˉj⋅−yˉk⋅)±LSD(\bar y_{j\cdot} - \bar y_{k\cdot}) \pm \mathrm{LSD} (Welham, p. 85–88).

On the example

Welham, Table 4.1: root length (cm) at four calcium concentrations, 5 pots each. Means: A 64.2, B 75.8, C 67.2, D 45.4; SEM 3.91, 95 % CI for A — 55.9…72.5. SED 5.53, LSD 11.72. The difference B − A = 11.6 is just below LSD: the CI -0.1…23.3 touches zero, P = 0.052 — as in the book. D differs from A, B and C (P: 0.004, < 0.001 and 0.001) — without a multiplicity adjustment; Tukey on the same data is on the page of Tukey's method.

Welham et al. (2015), p. 84–88.

Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths

  • More precise than the SD of each group: one s2s^2 from all the data, more df (Welham, p. 84–85).
  • A direct measure for comparison: one LSD bar next to the means says more than ± SD of each group (Welham, p. 87–88).
  • A CI of the difference, not just P (Welham, p. 85–88).
  • Unequal replication is allowed: each pair has its own SED (Welham, p. 85–86).

Weaknesses

  • No multiplicity adjustment: 15 independent tests at 5 % give at least one false finding with probability 0.54 (Welham, p. 195–196). With three or more groups the method warns about this [BioStat].
  • “Protection” by a significant F does not help: both a significant F without significant pairs and significant pairs with a non-significant F are possible (Welham, p. 198–199).
  • Requires homogeneous variances: the common s2s^2 is wrong if the spread of the groups differs (Welham, p. 84–85).
  • Without blocks only: for an experiment with blocks — the comparisons of means with a block column [BioStat].

Welham et al. (2015), p. 84–88, 195–199; [BioStat].

How the map chooses this method

Path to the leaf

Analyse a response›Continuous›Differences›Independent units›No blocks or complete blocks›One factor›Three or more groups›After ANOVA›Means, SED, LSD

The map offers the “After ANOVA” step (B5) after the leaves comparing several groups. Which comparisons to make is the user's choice: it depends on the question of the experiment, not on the data (Welham, p. 201–205). This leaf is for the table of means and one or two comparisons chosen in advance. With the role “block”, the map marks this leaf “no blocks” and gives the hint B5.4: the means, SED and LSD of the model with blocks are already given by ANOVA with blocks, and comparisons follow the model with blocks.

Hints of the step: B5.1 — the factor levels are numbers (doses): a trend instead of pairwise comparisons; B5.2 — the factor has a level named “control”: comparisons with the control by Dunnett.

What the map checks at this leaf

  • B5.1 (hint) The factor levels are numbers, not codes 1…k: they may be doses.
  • B5.2 (hint) There is a level named “control” (and at least three levels).
  • B5.4 (hint) A column has the role “block”: comparisons follow the model with blocks.

What next

If all pairs are needed — Tukey's method; against a control — Dunnett in the comparisons of means. If the goal is to show that there are no differences, that is a different task: the equivalence test (TOST); a non-significant difference does not prove the absence of differences (Welham, p. 253).
