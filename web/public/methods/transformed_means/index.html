ANOVA on a transformed response

Do the group means differ when the spread grows with the mean or the distribution is skewed to the right? The response is moved to another scale (most often log), ANOVA is done there, and the means and CIs are brought back to the original scale.

Idea

Mass, height and abundance often change by factors rather than by a fixed number of units: a group with mean 100 spreads more than a group with mean 30. The logarithm pulls large values together and stretches small ones — the variances even out, the right skewness goes away, and a multiplicative model becomes additive (Welham, p. 113–118).

F, SED and LSD are computed on the log scale. Only the means and the CI limits are brought back to the original scale: a mean on the log scale becomes the geometric mean of the group, a difference of means becomes a ratio (“how many times”), and LSD becomes a factor. SE and SED are not back-transformed: the transformation is nonlinear (Welham, p. 122–126).

Transformation of the response (c is the offset for zeros; in the example c = 0):

zij=log⁡10(yij+c)z_{ij} = \log_{10}(y_{ij} + c)

Other options: ln; √ — milder than the logarithm; logit — for proportions and percentages (Welham, p. 119–121). Offset for zeros: c = 1 for integer counts, otherwise half the smallest positive value (p. 116).

A back-transformed mean is a geometric mean: it is no larger than the arithmetic mean, and the gap grows with skewness. The CI limits are back-transformed the same way, so CIs on the original scale are asymmetric (Welham, p. 122–126).

Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths

  • Treats the cause, not the symptom: when the variance grows with the mean, a single transformation evens out the variances and removes the skewness (Welham, p. 113–118).
  • Ratios instead of differences: for log the result reads as “how many times”, with CIs (Welham, p. 123–126).
  • The whole ANOVA toolkit remains: F, SED, LSD and comparisons — on the new scale (Welham, p. 122).
  • The same transformation is available in RCBD and in the comparisons of means — a parameter in the method form [BioStat].

Weaknesses

  • The result is on another scale: geometric means are not arithmetic means, and SE and SED are not carried back to the original scale (Welham, p. 122–126).
  • The offset c for zeros affects the result (Welham, p. 116).
  • Sometimes no transformation fits: counts with many zeros, proportions from small samples or near 0 and 1 — a GLM is needed (Welham, p. 126–127).
  • Back-transformation with an offset can leave the scale (negative counts, proportions above 1) — BioStat warns (Welham, p. 122).
  • The method's pairwise ratios have no multiplicity adjustment (LSD) [BioStat].

Welham et al. (2015), p. 113–127; [BioStat].

How the map chooses this method

Path to the leaf

Analyse a response›Continuous›Differences›Independent units›No blocks or complete blocks›One factor›Three or more groups›Transform the response›ANOVA on log / √ / logit scale

The map leads here with signal B4.3: the group SDs grow with the means — the extreme SDs differ at least twofold, and the correlation of log SD with log mean is r ≥ 0.8 [BioStat]. From four groups this is a strong signal; with three, a hint: over three points such a link is often accidental [BioStat: audit 01-07]. With an outlier candidate, B4.3 becomes a hint.

Hints: B4.4 — residuals are strongly skewed (|g₁| > 1); R2.7 — all values are greater than 0 and the skewness of the response is above 1 [BioStat]. For two groups the same signals lead to the leaf “Comparison on the log / √ / logit scale” — it is the same method. In a complete block experiment (role “block”), B4.3 and B4.4 lead not here but to ANOVA with blocks: there the transformation is a parameter of the method.

  • B4.3 SDs grow with the means (four groups or more) — a strong signal [BioStat].
  • B4.3 (hint) The same with three groups — only a hint: over three points such a link is often accidental [BioStat: audit 01-07].
  • B4.4 (hint) / R2.7 (hint) Strong skewness of residuals or of a positive response — a reason to try log.
  • B4.2 / B4.5 Heterogeneous variances (Bartlett) without a link between SD and the means lead not here but to Welch (B4.5).

What next

The method itself gives all pairs: ratios with CIs without a multiplicity adjustment. For comparisons with an adjustment (Tukey, Dunnett) — the comparisons of means of the “After ANOVA” step with the same transformation in the method form [BioStat]. If the goal is to show that there are no differences, that is a different task: the equivalence test (TOST) (Welham, p. 253).
