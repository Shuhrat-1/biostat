One-way ANOVA

Do the means of three or more groups differ — for example, the yields of several varieties? The answer comes from comparing two kinds of spread: between the groups and within them.

Idea

Look at the points below: the yields of three varieties, 6 plots each. Each point deviates from the grand mean for two reasons: because its variety differs on average (the between segment, from the variety mean to the grand mean) and because of plot-to-plot chance (the within segment, from the point to the mean of its own variety).

If the varieties are really the same, their means scatter only by that same chance, and “between” is comparable in size to “within”. If “between” is much larger, the differences are real. ANOVA measures this ratio and calls it F.

The F statistic

F=MSBMSW=SSB/(t−1)SSW/(N−t)=5.53/20.85/15=2.7650.0567=48.8,P=2.7⋅10−7F = \frac{MS_B}{MS_W} = \frac{SS_B/(t-1)}{SS_W/(N-t)} = \frac{5.53/2}{0.85/15} = \frac{2.765}{0.0567} = 48.8,\qquad P = 2.7\cdot10^{-7}

If H0H_0 is true and the assumptions hold, F follows the F distribution with (t − 1, N − t) degrees of freedom. P is the share of that distribution to the right of the observed F.

Means, SED and confidence intervals

The residual mean square MSWMS_W is the pooled estimate of σ² over all groups. The standard errors of the means, the standard error of the difference (SED) and the intervals are built on it:

Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths

  • Uses all the data at once. One estimate of σ² from all groups: N − t degrees of freedom instead of n − 1 in each pair — more power than a series of t-tests, and no inflation of the Type I error.
  • Gives more than P. Means with CIs, SED and LSD for comparisons — all on the common MSWMS_W.
  • Robust to moderate departures from normality, especially in a randomized experiment.
  • The basis of all linear models. The same partition of SS carries over to blocks, factorials and split plots.
  • Two groups are a special case: F = t² of Student's two-sample t-test.

Weaknesses

  • Sensitive to unequal variances when the n are unequal. If the larger variance is in the smaller group, P is too small [proposed: Glass et al. 1972].
  • A single outlier inflates MSWMS_W and shifts the mean.
  • Answers only “are there any differences at all”. Which groups differ is the next step: comparisons.
  • Wrong with dependent observations. Blocks and pseudo-replicates analyzed as independent give false precision.
  • Not for proportions, counts or scores — for them, a GLM or rank methods.

Welham et al. (2015), p. 33, 69, 75, 83, 84, 86, 102–107, 127.

How the map chooses this method

Path to the leaf

Analyse a response›Continuous›Differences›Independent units›No blocks or complete blocks›One factor›Three or more groups›One-way ANOVA

First the map establishes the structure of the experiment (whether measurements are linked, whether there are blocks, how many factors) and only then the assumptions. A one-way analysis with ignored blocks is wrong whatever the normality (Welham, p. 69), so this order never changes.

ANOVA is the default method in its branch. There is no separate “green” signal for it: if no assumption rule fires, the map leaves ANOVA without remarks and the neighbors without highlighting.

What the map checks at this leaf

  • DIAG.1 Outlier first. If one point stands out sharply (Bonferroni correction), the Welch, log and permutation-test advice is demoted to a hint: “check the record in row N first”. The method is not changed because of an outlier (Welham, p. 109–110).
  • B4.1 Normality of residuals (Shapiro–Wilk); with n > 30 in every group, only together with skewness > 0.5 [BioStat].
  • B4.2 / B4.3 Homogeneity of variances and the link between SD and mean — lead to Welch or to a transformation.
  • B4.4 (hint) Strong skewness of residuals (|g₁| > 1) — a reason to transform.
  • DIAG.3 Fewer than 10 residual df — a warning about the low power of the comparisons.

What next

A significant F says only that the means are not all equal. The next step on the map is “After ANOVA: which comparisons?”: pre-planned contrasts, a dose trend, all pairs by Tukey, against a control by Dunnett, planned pairs by Holm. If the goal is to show that there are no differences, that is a different task: the equivalence test (TOST); a non-significant F does not prove the absence of differences (Welham, p. 253).
