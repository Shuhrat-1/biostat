[BioStat] thresholds

Thresholds of map hints and method rules that are not in the books are BioStat heuristics:

  • Normality of residuals (B4.1, B4.7): with more than 30 observations in every group, only marked skewness, |g₁| > 0.5, counts as a violation; with smaller groups the Shapiro–Wilk test decides.
  • Normality of paired differences (B0.6) is checked from 6 pairs on: with fewer the test detects almost nothing.
  • Two groups (B4.2, two_sample_t): Student's t by default; Welch is advised when the SD ratio is > 2 with balanced n, or > 1.5 when the n ratio exceeds 1.5; with n < 8 in a group it is a note only.
  • SD grows with the mean (B4.3, a cue for a log transformation): r between log SD and log mean of the groups ≥ 0.8 with SDs differing at least twofold; a confident signal from 4 groups on.
  • Skewed residuals (B4.4): |g₁| > 1 (from 8 residuals) — a hint to transform the response.
  • Skewed response (R2.7): g₁ > 1 with all values > 0 (from 8 observations) — a candidate for a log transformation.
  • Looks like counts (R2.2): non-negative integers, not only 0 and 1, minimum ≤ 10 (otherwise more likely integer measurements such as grams).
  • Many zeros in the response (R2.6): a hint from a 30 % share of zeros, a firm pointer from 50 %.
  • Spread of counts (G.2): variance more than twice the mean (from 10 observations) — a negative binomial model may fit.
  • Overdispersion in a GLM (G.1): the deviance is significantly larger than its degrees of freedom and deviance/df > 1.2.
  • Looks like scores (B0.4, B4.8): integers, 3 to 10 distinct values, range at most 10 — the map suggests rank methods.
  • Numeric class codes (R2.11): 3–10 consecutive integers from 0 or 1, on average at least 5 rows per code; more than 10 distinct values are more likely a measurement than treatment numbers.
  • Possible repeated measures (B0.2): two numeric columns on one scale (ratio of means 0.5 to 2) and r > 0.7 over 5 or more pairs.
  • Repeated measures from roles: every subject is measured at two or more times, at most once at each time.
  • Random factor (B1.9): with fewer than 5 levels the variance component is estimated poorly — the map suggests a fixed block.
  • Curvature of a straight line (A2.3): the quadratic term is significant (p < 0.05) with 4 or more distinct x.
  • Spread grows with the fitted value (A2.4): Spearman ρ > 0.5 and p < 0.05, from 10 points.
  • Outlying residuals (DIAG.1): the share of |r| > 2 is clearly above the expected 5 %, or a residual exceeds the Bonferroni limit (at least 3).
  • Serial correlation of residuals (DIAG.2): Durbin–Watson outside (1, 3), checked from 15 observations on — with 10–12 the statistic leaves these limits by chance in 8–13 % of cases.
  • Influential points in regression (DIAG.4): leverage above 2p/N and above 0.2; influence — Cook's distance above F₀.₅(p, N − p).
  • Exact P of rank tests with ties comes from the permutation distribution; it is computed up to 200 non-zero differences (signed-rank test) and up to 100 observations (Mann–Whitney).
  • Separation of categories in ordinal regression: complete — log L > −10⁻⁶·n; partial — |β| > 10 with SE > 100.
  • Factorial ANOVA: an interaction left out of the model with P < 0.10 is flagged as borderline.
  • ANCOVA: lack of fit is not tested when there are more than 200 distinct x (x is continuous).
  • Regression with groups: a warning when the x ranges of two groups overlap by less than half.
