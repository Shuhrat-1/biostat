Two-sample t-test

Do the means of two groups differ — for example, the yields of two varieties? The t-test divides the difference of means by its standard error and asks whether it is large for chance alone.

Idea

A difference of means says nothing by itself: 0.5 t/ha is a lot if plots of one variety differ by 0.1, and little if they differ by 2. So the difference is divided by its standard error SED, which grows with the spread within groups and falls with the number of observations (Welham, p. 32–34).

The result is t — how many standard errors fit into the difference. If the varieties are really the same, large |t| are rare; P is the probability of a |t| at least as large as the observed one. For wheat and barley, a difference of 0.55 t/ha with SED 0.15 gives t = 3.67.

(yˉ1⋅−yˉ2⋅)±t0.975; N−t SED=(0.22; 0.88),d=yˉ1⋅−yˉ2⋅sp=2.12(\bar y_{1\cdot}-\bar y_{2\cdot}) \pm t_{0.975;\,N-t}\,\mathrm{SED} = (0.22;\ 0.88),\qquad d = \frac{\bar y_{1\cdot}-\bar y_{2\cdot}}{s_p} = 2.12

With two groups, N−t=n1+n2−2N-t = n_1+n_2-2. Hedges' gg is the same dd corrected for small-sample bias: 1.95 [proposed: Hedges 1981].

Welch version

When the variances are unequal they are not pooled: each group gives its own error, and the degrees of freedom are estimated from the data (Welham, p. 33; Witte, p. 266–267):

tW=yˉ1⋅−yˉ2⋅s12/n1+s22/n2,ν=(s12/n1+s22/n2)2(s12/n1)2n1−1+(s22/n2)2n2−1t_W = \frac{\bar y_{1\cdot}-\bar y_{2\cdot}}{\sqrt{s_1^2/n_1 + s_2^2/n_2}},\qquad \nu = \frac{\left(s_1^2/n_1 + s_2^2/n_2\right)^2}{\dfrac{(s_1^2/n_1)^2}{n_1-1} + \dfrac{(s_2^2/n_2)^2}{n_2-1}}

On the example

Wheat 5.30, barley 4.75 t/ha (6 plots each); difference 0.55, SED 0.15, t = 3.67 on 10 df, P = 0.0043; 95 % CI of the difference 0.22…0.88; d = 2.12, g = 1.95. The Welch rule did not fire — Student version; Levene (for reference): P = 0.36.

Welham et al. (2015), p. 32–35; Witte & Witte (2017), p. 244–256 (t), 259–264 (CI and dd), 266–267 (Welch), 308 (F=t2F = t^2); gg — [proposed: Hedges 1981].

Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths

  • Answers the two-group question directly. The difference of means with SED and 95 % CI is what the book treats as the main result (Welham, p. 34–35).
  • Robust with equal n. With equal groups of more than ~10 observations, moderate departures from the assumptions hardly matter (Witte, p. 266).
  • The Welch version removes the requirement of equal variances (Welham, p. 33; Witte, p. 266–267).
  • Gives an effect size: Cohen's dd and Hedges' gg (Witte, p. 261–264; [proposed: Hedges 1981]).
  • Has a directional version: a one-sided test, if the direction is set before the experiment (Witte, p. 195–202).

Weaknesses

  • Two groups only. For three or more — one-way ANOVA with a single estimate of σ² from all groups (Welham, p. 74–84).
  • Unequal variances with unequal n: when the larger variance is in the smaller group, Student's P is too small [BioStat; proposed: Ruxton 2006; Zimmerman 2004].
  • A single outlier shifts the mean and inflates SED (Welham, p. 109–110).
  • Wrong for linked measurements: two measurements of the same unit call for the paired t-test (Welham, p. 41).
  • With small n (below ~10) departures from normality are dangerous and almost impossible to detect (Witte, p. 387).

Welham et al. (2015), p. 33–35, 41, 74–84, 109–110; Witte & Witte (2017), p. 195–202, 261–267, 387; the Welch rule — [BioStat; proposed: Ruxton 2006; Zimmerman 2004].

How the map chooses this method

Path to the leaf

Analyse a response›Continuous›Differences›Independent units›No blocks or complete blocks›One factor›Two groups›t-test

The map leads here when the units are independent and the single factor has two levels. The t-test is the default method in the “Two groups” branch: there is no positive signal for it.

Unequal variances do not move you off this leaf: signal B4.2 suggests the same t-test in its Welch version (the version is chosen in the method form). If the SDs differ because of right skewness with non-normal residuals, B4.2 stays silent: then B4.1 and B4.4 lead to ranks or a transformation.

What the map checks at this leaf

  • DIAG.1 Outlier first. With an outlier candidate, the Welch advice is demoted to a hint “check the record in row N first” (Welham, p. 109–110).
  • B4.1 Normality of the residuals of the two-group model; with n > 30 in both groups, only together with skewness > 0.5 [BioStat]. Leads to Mann–Whitney.
  • B4.2 Unequal SDs — the same t-test in the Welch version; with n < 8 in a group, for reference only [BioStat].
  • B4.4 (hint) Strong skewness of residuals (|g₁| > 1) — a reason to transform.
  • B4.7 (hint) All groups have n > 30: a departure from normality is probably harmless (CLT heuristic) [BioStat].
  • DIAG.3 Fewer than 10 residual df — a warning about low power.

What next

A significant t says that the means differ; the size of the difference is shown by the difference itself and its 95 % CI — these are what to report (Welham, p. 34–35). If the goal is to show that there are no differences, that is a different task: the equivalence test (TOST); a non-significant t does not prove the absence of differences (Welham, p. 253).
