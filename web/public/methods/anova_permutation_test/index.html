Permutation F-test (ANOVA)

Do the group means differ when the residuals are not normal? The permutation test uses the same F as ANOVA but gets P by shuffling the group labels rather than from the F distribution.

Idea

If the treatment has no effect on the response, a unit's group label means nothing: in a randomized experiment, any allocation of the labels to the units was equally possible. The test shuffles the labels many times, recomputes F∗F^* each time and checks how often a random allocation gives an F at least as large as the observed one (Welham, p. 102–103).

The F distribution is not needed — the distribution of the F∗F^* values themselves replaces it. So normality is not required, but homogeneity of variances still is: shuffling assumes that the groups differ only by a shift (Welham, p. 102).

The statistic — the same F

F=MSBMSW=27.8,df=(2; 27)F = \frac{MS_B}{MS_W} = 27.8,\qquad df = (2;\ 27)

P from random permutations

P=1+#{ b: Fb∗≥F }B+1,B=999,P=0.001P = \frac{1 + \#\{\,b:\ F^*_b \ge F\,\}}{B+1},\qquad B = 999,\quad P = 0.001

The one added to the numerator and the denominator counts the observed data as one of the allocations, so P is never smaller than 1/(B+1)1/(B+1) [proposed: Phipson & Smyth 2010]. If there are no more distinct allocations than B, BioStat enumerates all of them, and P is the exact share of allocations with F∗≥FF^* \ge F (the observed one among them), with no one added (Welham, p. 102–103).

On the example

Illustrative data set (map scenario S09): three groups of 10 values of the same right-skewed shape, shifted by 5, 6 and 8 — the variances are equal (Levene, for reference: P = 1.00), the residuals are non-normal, and the map gives B4.6. F = 27.8 on (2; 27) df. None of the 999 permutations (seed = 1) gave F∗≥F^* \ge 27.8, so P = 0.001 — the smallest possible with B = 999; from the F distribution, P = 2.8·10⁻⁷. There are 5 550 996 791 340 allocations of 30 values to three groups, so the permutations are random. On this set the map also hints at a transformation (B4.4, residual skewness above 1); the DIAG.2 hint about correlation along the row order arises because the values in the set are sorted.

Welham et al. (2015), p. 102–103; the formula for P — [proposed: Phipson & Smyth 2010].

Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths

  • Does not need normal residuals — P comes from the distribution of F∗F^* (Welham, p. 102).
  • The same statistic and the same means as in ANOVA: the results compare directly [BioStat].
  • Exact when there are few allocations: if there are no more than B, all of them are enumerated (Welham, p. 102–103).
  • Reproducible: the seed is stored in the result, and a recalculation gives the same P [BioStat].

Weaknesses

  • Needs homogeneous variances — with heterogeneous ones it is no substitute for Welch (Welham, p. 102).
  • Justified by randomization: with observational data, shuffling the labels is only an approximation (Welham, p. 103).
  • P is never below 1/(B+1)1/(B+1) with random permutations and changes slightly with another seed; very small P need more permutations (B up to 99 999) [BioStat].
  • One factor without blocks only. The permutations must respect the structure of the experiment (Welham, p. 102–103); BioStat has no such version for blocks.
  • Does not fix an outlier: F is still built on the means (Welham, p. 109–110).

Welham et al. (2015), p. 102–103, 109–110; [BioStat].

How the map chooses this method

Path to the leaf

Analyse a response›Continuous›Differences›Independent units›No blocks or complete blocks›One factor›Three or more groups›Permutation test

The map leads here with signal B4.6: in three or more groups the residuals are non-normal (B4.1) while the variances are homogeneous (B4.2 is silent). If the variances are heterogeneous, B4.6 stays silent, and the choice is between Welch and a transformation.

For two groups the map has no permutation-test leaf: non-normal residuals lead to Mann–Whitney. The method itself accepts two groups. With an outlier candidate, B4.6 becomes a hint: check the record first.

What the map checks at this leaf

  • DIAG.1 Outlier first. With an outlier candidate, the permutation-test advice is demoted to a hint “check the record in row N first” (Welham, p. 109–110).
  • B4.1 Normality of residuals (Shapiro–Wilk); with n > 30 in every group, only together with skewness > 0.5 [BioStat].
  • B4.2 Homogeneity of variances (Bartlett): if heterogeneous — Welch or a transformation instead of this test.
  • B4.3 / B4.4 (hint) A link between SD and the means and strong skewness of residuals — reasons to transform.
  • DIAG.3 Fewer than 10 residual df — a warning about low power.

What next

The permutation P answers whether the means differ at all. The comparisons after ANOVA (B5) use the t distribution: with clearly non-normal residuals their P are approximate; BioStat has no permutation version of the comparisons. If the goal is to show that there are no differences, that is a different task: the equivalence test (TOST); a non-significant F does not prove the absence of differences (Welham, p. 253).
